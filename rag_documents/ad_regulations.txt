# Global Advertising Regulations Summary

## 1. Truth in Advertising (FTC Guidelines - United States)
- All advertising claims must be truthful and non-deceptive.
- Advertisers must have evidence to back up their claims ("substantiation").
- Ads cannot be unfair, meaning they cannot cause substantial consumer injury.
- Endorsements must reflect honest opinions. If a connection exists between the endorser and the seller, it must be disclosed.
- The FTC Act prohibits "unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce."
- Native advertising and sponsored content must be clearly labeled as advertising.

## 2. ASA CAP Code (United Kingdom)
- Marketing communications must be legal, decent, honest, and truthful.
- Marketing communications must be prepared with a sense of responsibility to consumers and to society.
- No marketing communication should bring advertising into disrepute.
- Marketing communications must not mislead or be likely to mislead by inaccuracy, ambiguity, exaggeration, omission, or otherwise.
- Subjective claims must not be presented as objective claims.
- Before distributing or submitting a marketing communication for publication, marketers must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective.

## 3. Health & Medical Claims
- Health claims must be backed by rigorous scientific evidence.
- No product may claim to cure, treat, or prevent disease without regulatory approval.
- Before-and-after images must not be misleading.
- Weight loss ads must not promise specific results without evidence.
- Supplements cannot claim to replace medical treatment.

## 4. Financial Products Advertising
- All financial promotions must be fair, clear, and not misleading.
- Risk warnings must be displayed prominently, not hidden in small print.
- APR and interest rates must be shown clearly.
- Past performance disclaimers are required: "Past performance is not indicative of future results."
- Cryptocurrency ads must include risk warnings about potential total loss.

## 5. Alcohol Advertising
- Alcohol ads must not be directed at people under the legal drinking age.
- Ads must not imply that alcohol enhances social, sexual, or physical capabilities.
- Ads must not encourage excessive or irresponsible consumption.
- Ads must not link alcohol to driving or operating machinery.
- Health warnings may be required depending on jurisdiction.

## 6. Advertising to Children
- Ads must not exploit children's credulity, loyalty, vulnerability, or lack of experience.
- Ads must not directly exhort children to buy advertised products or persuade parents to buy.
- Ads for high-fat, high-sugar, high-salt (HFSS) foods have placement restrictions.
- COPPA (US): Cannot collect personal data from children under 13 without parental consent.
- In-app purchase mechanics must not be designed to exploit children.

## 7. Data Privacy in Advertising (GDPR / CCPA)
- Users must give explicit consent before their data is used for targeted advertising.
- Users have the right to opt out of personalized ads.
- Data collected for advertising must be stored securely and deleted when no longer needed.
- Third-party data sharing for ad targeting requires transparency and consent.
- Cookie consent banners are required in the EU for tracking technologies.

## 8. Comparative Advertising
- Comparing products is allowed but must be factually accurate.
- Comparisons must not denigrate or discredit competitors unfairly.
- Claims of superiority must be substantiated with evidence.
- Comparisons must be between products meeting the same needs.
- Trademarks of competitors can be used for comparison but not to create confusion.

## 9. Environmental / Green Claims
- Environmental claims must be based on the full life cycle of the product.
- Vague terms like "eco-friendly" or "green" must be substantiated.
- Carbon-neutral claims must be supported by certified offset programs.
- Packaging claims (e.g., "recyclable") must reflect actual recycling infrastructure.
- Greenwashing is subject to regulatory action in the EU, UK, and US.

## 10. Influencer & Social Media Advertising
- Sponsored content must be clearly disclosed using labels like #ad or #sponsored.
- Disclosure must be immediate and prominent, not buried in hashtags.
- Influencers must actually use products they endorse.
- Fake reviews and fake followers are prohibited.
- Platforms may be held liable for undisclosed paid partnerships.
