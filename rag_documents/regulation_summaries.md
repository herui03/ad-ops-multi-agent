## [truth-in-advertising] Truth in advertising (United States, FTC framework, paraphrased)
Advertising claims must be truthful and not deceptive, and advertisers need evidence to substantiate objective claims. Endorsements must reflect honest opinions and material connections must be disclosed.

## [cap-code] UK CAP Code (paraphrased)
Marketing communications must be legal, decent, honest and truthful, must not mislead by exaggeration or omission, and marketers must hold evidence for claims consumers would regard as objective.

## [health-claims] Health and medical claims (paraphrased)
Products must not claim to cure, treat or prevent disease without regulatory approval, and before-and-after images must not mislead.

## [financial-promotions] Financial promotions (paraphrased)
Financial promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading, with prominent risk warnings and clearly shown rates.

## [children] Advertising to children (paraphrased)
Ads must not exploit children's inexperience or directly urge children to buy or to pester parents.

## [privacy] Data privacy in advertising (GDPR and CCPA, paraphrased)
Consent is generally required before personal data is used for targeted advertising in the EU, and users must be able to opt out of personalised ads.

## [comparative] Comparative advertising (paraphrased)
Comparisons with competitors must be factually accurate, compare like with like, and not denigrate competitors. Superiority claims need substantiation.

## [green-claims] Environmental claims (paraphrased)
Vague terms such as "eco-friendly" must be substantiated, and carbon-neutral claims need a credible offset basis.

## [influencer] Influencer disclosure (paraphrased)
Sponsored content must be clearly and immediately disclosed, for example with an "ad" label.
